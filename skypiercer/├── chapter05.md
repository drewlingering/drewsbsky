The reactor chamber lay deep within Skypiercer's spine — a labyrinth of reinforced corridors, radiation‑shielded bulkheads, and humming conduits that pulsed like arteries. Snowpiercer's boarding party followed Lysandra through the final security gate, the air growing warmer with each step. The hum of the nuclear core vibrated through the floor, steady but... strained.

Bennett Knox felt it immediately.

Reactors weren't supposed to sound like they were breathing.

The chamber doors opened, revealing the heart of Skypiercer: a massive cylindrical reactor suspended within a magnetic cradle. Blue light flickered unevenly across its surface, the containment field rippling like disturbed water.

Jace froze. He had grown up hearing stories of the reactor — the sacred engine that kept Skypiercer alive, the source of heat, motion, and survival. But he had never seen it. No Groundless ever had.

And now, seeing it up close, he realised something was wrong.

Very wrong.

The Hidden Failure

Bennett approached the diagnostic console. The interface was unfamiliar, but the readings were universal — temperature spikes, containment fluctuations, fuel degradation curves. He scrolled through the logs, each line confirming the same truth.

The reactor was failing. Not slowly. Not hypothetically.

Catastrophically.

"Commander," Bennett said quietly, "your core is entering cascade instability."

Lysandra's jaw tightened. "We suspected as much."

Ruth turned sharply. "You suspected? You brought us up here without telling us your ark is about to fall out of the sky?"

Lysandra didn't flinch. "The Aviators believed revealing the truth would cause panic."

Jace stared at her, stunned. "You hid this from us? From everyone?"

Lysandra met his gaze. "We had no choice."

But Bennett shook his head. "You had a choice. You just didn't like the consequences."

He pointed to the containment field. "This reactor has maybe weeks before the cradle fails. When it does, Skypiercer won't glide. It'll drop. Straight down."

The words hung in the chamber like a death sentence.

The Aviators' Secret

Lysandra activated a restricted log — one Snowpiercer's crew hadn't been meant to see. The hologram displayed a series of internal reports, each stamped with Aviator clearance.

Fuel degradation accelerating. Containment field weakening. Magnetic cradle losing stability.

And then the final line:

"Do not disclose reactor status to Floaters or Groundless. Maintain doctrine."

Ruth exhaled sharply. "They kept your entire society in the dark."

Jace felt his stomach twist. All his life, he had believed in the Never‑Landing Doctrine. All his life, he had trusted the Aviators. All his life, he had been told the sky was safety.

But now he understood the truth:

The sky was dying.

The Plan

Bennett stepped back from the console, thinking fast. "Your reactor can't be repaired with what you have onboard. But Snowpiercer has geothermal stabilisers — systems designed to regulate extreme heat fluctuations."

Lysandra frowned. "You're suggesting we tether Skypiercer to Snowpiercer's power grid?"

"Not the grid," Bennett said. "The stabiliser array. If we link your reactor to our geothermal regulators, we can bleed off the excess heat and reinforce your containment field."

Ruth crossed her arms. "That's assuming the tether holds."

"It will," Bennett said. "If we anchor it properly."

Jace looked between them. "You can save us?"

Bennett nodded. "We can stabilise your reactor. But we'll need access to every system the Aviators have locked down."

Lysandra's expression hardened. "That won't be easy."

Ruth stepped forward. "Commander, your ark is going to crash. Thousands will die. Easy isn't the priority."

Lysandra closed her eyes for a moment, then opened them with resolve.

"Then we break the locks."

The First Breach

The reactor chamber alarms suddenly flared — a containment dip, brief but sharp. The cradle shuddered, sending a tremor through the floor.

Jace grabbed a railing. "What was that?"

Bennett checked the readings. "A warning. The core is entering early-stage cascade."

Ruth looked at Lysandra. "We don't have weeks. We have days."

Lysandra turned to her officers. "Prepare the tether bay. Snowpiercer's stabilisers must be connected immediately."

Her voice echoed through the chamber, carrying a weight that shattered doctrine, hierarchy, and secrecy in a single command.

The truth was out.

The reactor was failing. The Aviators had lied. And the only hope for Skypiercer... was Snowpiercer.
