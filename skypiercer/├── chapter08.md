The tether screamed under the strain of the storm, vibrating like a living thing as Skypiercer descended through the final layer of cloud. Snowpiercer held position beneath it, engines roaring, geothermal stabilisers glowing with molten intensity. The storm clawed at both arks — lightning tearing across the sky, ice slamming against metal, winds howling with the fury of a world that had forgotten how to be gentle.

But the tether held.

And Skypiercer continued its descent.

For seven years, the megacraft had lived by a single rule: If we land, we die. That doctrine had shaped its society, its hierarchy, its fears. But now, with the reactor stabilised by Snowpiercer's systems and the storm rising like a ghost from the past, the doctrine was no longer a warning.

It was a challenge.

Lysandra Vale stood at the command gallery window, watching the frozen world rise toward her. "Prepare landing struts," she ordered. Her voice trembled — not with fear, but with awe.

The struts extended, massive reinforced legs unfolding beneath Skypiercer's belly. The megacraft groaned as its weight shifted, the magnetic cradle adjusting to compensate for gravity it had not felt in years.

Jace pressed his hands against the glass, eyes wide. "That's... ground. Real ground."

Bennett's voice crackled through the comm from Snowpiercer. "Landing zone stable. We're holding the storm back as long as we can."

Ruth shouted over the wind. "Bring her down!"

Touchdown

The storm struck first — a wall of ice and wind slamming into the landing zone. Snowpiercer's stabilisers flared, creating a dome of heat that melted the worst of the freeze. The ground beneath Skypiercer glowed faintly, protected by geothermal pulses.

Then Skypiercer touched down.

A deep, resonant impact rolled across the frozen plain. The megacraft's struts sank into the ice, anchoring it firmly. The entire structure shuddered, adjusting, settling, accepting the impossible:

Skypiercer had landed.

For the first time in seven years. Against doctrine. Against fear. Against history.

The storm howled around the stabiliser dome, but it could not break through. Snowpiercer's systems held firm, shielding both arks from the flash‑freeze that had once killed the first landing team.

Inside Skypiercer, silence fell.

Not fear. Not panic. Something else.

Wonder.

The First Steps

The tether‑module doors opened, releasing a wave of warm air into the frozen landscape. Snowpiercer's crew stepped out first, boots crunching against the ice. Ruth inhaled deeply, the cold biting her lungs.

Then Skypiercer's people emerged.

Aviators. Floaters. Groundless.

Some stepped cautiously, others ran forward, falling to their knees to touch the ground they had only heard stories about. Jace walked slowly, his breath visible in the air, his eyes wide with disbelief.

"It's real," he whispered. "The world... it's real."

Lysandra stepped beside him, her boots sinking into the snow. "We survived in the sky," she said softly. "But we were never meant to stay there forever."

Snowpiercer's crew and Skypiercer's survivors stood together beneath the storm‑lit sky — two arks, two histories, two societies meeting on the frozen earth for the first time.

The doctrine was broken. The truth was revealed. The future was unwritten.

The Transmission

Bennett adjusted his comm unit, trying to stabilise the tether link. Static crackled through the channel — faint, distant, irregular. He frowned, tapping the receiver.

"Snowpiercer to Skypiercer, do you copy?"

Static.

Then a voice.

Weak. Metallic. Far away.

"Skybridge Station... online."

Ruth froze. "What was that?"

Bennett replayed the signal. The same words. The same voice.

"Skybridge Station... online."

Lysandra looked toward the horizon, where the storm clouds parted just enough to reveal a faint glimmer of light — not natural, not atmospheric.

Artificial.

A structure. A beacon. A third ark.

Jace whispered the words as if afraid they might vanish.

"Skybridge... is real."

The storm roared behind them. The two arks stood united on the frozen ground. And somewhere in the distance, another survivor called out across the dead world.

The landing was only the beginning.
