CHAPTER 7 — DESCENT
The reactor alarms hammered through Skypiercer’s decks like a heartbeat gone wild. Red lights strobed across the command gallery, casting frantic shadows over the Aviators scrambling at their stations. The containment field had dipped twice in the last hour — each time deeper than the last.

Bennett Knox stared at the readings, jaw tight.
“It’s happening. The cradle can’t hold her at altitude anymore.”

Lysandra Vale didn’t argue. She had known this moment was coming ever since Snowpiercer exposed the truth. The reactor was failing, and Skypiercer — the ark that had sworn never to land — was about to fall.

“Prepare for controlled descent,” she ordered.

The words struck the gallery like a shockwave.
Aviators froze.
Floaters stared.
Groundless whispered in disbelief.

Controlled descent.
The unthinkable.
The forbidden.

The doctrine shattered in a single command.

The Drop Begins
The megacraft shuddered as the first descent thrusters engaged. Skypiercer’s colossal wings adjusted, angling downward, slicing through the thinning stratosphere. The air outside roared, buffeting the hull with turbulence it hadn’t felt in seven years.

Jace gripped a railing, eyes wide.
“We’re… falling.”

“Descending,” Lysandra corrected, though her voice betrayed the fear beneath the word.

The floor tilted slightly as Skypiercer dipped below the cloud line. For the first time in his life, Jace saw the world not as a distant myth, but as a rising landscape — jagged ice ridges, frozen plains, storm fronts swirling like living beasts.

Snowpiercer was down there.
Waiting.
Racing to meet them.

Snowpiercer Moves
On the ground, Snowpiercer thundered across the ice, its engines pushed to their limits. Ruth Wardell stood at the front of the train, wind whipping her coat as she watched Skypiercer’s silhouette grow larger in the sky.

“Faster!” she shouted to the engineers. “We need to be directly beneath them before the reactor dips again!”

The tether‑module anchors had been prepared — reinforced, stabilised, ready to link Skypiercer’s failing reactor to Snowpiercer’s geothermal stabilisers. But positioning was everything. If Snowpiercer wasn’t under Skypiercer at the exact moment the tether deployed, the connection would fail.

And Skypiercer would crash.

Bennett’s voice crackled through the comm.
“Storm front forming. Same signature as the flash‑freeze event from the doctrine.”

Ruth’s stomach dropped.
The storm that killed the first landing team.
The storm that birthed the Never‑Landing Doctrine.
The storm that Skypiercer feared above all else.

It was forming again.

The Storm Rises
Clouds churned violently around Skypiercer as it descended. Lightning arced across the sky, illuminating the megacraft’s massive frame. The temperature plummeted — fast, unnatural, catastrophic.

Bennett checked the readings.
“This isn’t natural. Something’s amplifying it.”

Lysandra’s eyes narrowed.
“The reactor emissions?”

“Partly,” Bennett said. “But there’s another factor. Atmospheric instability triggered by descent. The doctrine wasn’t entirely wrong.”

The storm roared, a wall of ice and wind rising to swallow Skypiercer whole.

Jace stared at the swirling vortex.
“This is what killed them. The first landing team.”

Lysandra placed a hand on his shoulder.
“And it will kill us too… unless Snowpiercer gets into position.”

The Alignment
Snowpiercer surged forward, its engine car glowing with geothermal heat. The train’s stabiliser arrays powered up, sending pulses of energy into the air — a beacon for Skypiercer’s tether systems.

Ruth watched the sky, shouting into the comm.
“Lysandra, we’re in position! Deploy the tether!”

Above, Skypiercer’s tether bay opened, releasing a massive reinforced cable that unfurled toward the ground. It whipped violently in the storm winds, stabiliser fins fighting to keep it aligned.

“Hold her steady!” Lysandra commanded.

The megacraft groaned, metal straining under the storm’s fury.

The tether dropped lower.
Lower.
Lower.

Snowpiercer’s anchor arms extended, reaching upward.

The storm screamed.

The reactor pulsed.

The tether connected.

A blinding surge of energy shot through both arks — Snowpiercer’s geothermal stabilisers linking with Skypiercer’s failing core.

Bennett shouted over the comm.
“Stabilisation holding! We’ve got them!”

The Descent Continues
With the tether locked, Skypiercer’s descent slowed. The megacraft steadied, its wings adjusting, its reactor stabilising under Snowpiercer’s support.

But the storm wasn’t finished.

Lightning struck the tether.
Ice slammed against Skypiercer’s hull.
Wind tore at Snowpiercer’s anchor arms.

Ruth braced herself.
“Hold the line!”

The two arks — one of the sky, one of the ground — moved together, fighting the storm as a single machine.

The doctrine had said landing meant death.

But now, for the first time, landing meant survival.
